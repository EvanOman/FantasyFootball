How much do top players matter?

Evan’s hypothesis is that collecting the most top-30 or top-40 players matters more than the rest of the draft. These three views test the reasoning: how value falls with draft rank, how much early picks capture each year, and how well preseason ranks identify the eventual top performers.

Here, value means positive PPR points above a position’s replacement player, after filling the league’s 64 offensive starting slots. Ranks 1–10 averaged 4.6 per scheduled game; ranks 31–40 averaged 2.3.

Early ranks carry more value on average. These results support weighting them more heavily, but do not establish top-30 or top-40 counts as the strongest predictor of team results.

All three charts use nflverse PPR results and Fantasy Football Calculator’s 12-team two-QB ADP. This historical price proxy differs from the 2026 ESPN ranking below. Definitions and limitations.

Sources, rules, and calculation details

Historical value

For each completed 2016–2025 regular season, the league-wide optimizer fills eight QB, sixteen RB, sixteen WR, eight TE, eight FLEX (RB/WR/TE), and eight SUPERFLEX slots. The next unselected player at each position defines replacement. Positive value is max(0, season PPR points − replacement points), divided by 16 scheduled games through 2020 and 17 thereafter. This includes the final NFL week and excludes playoffs. It is a full-season starter baseline, not a prediction of weekly waiver or streaming value.

Each season supplies 140 offensive preseason selections in ordinal ADP order. Injured and zero-production players remain in the sample. All 2,800 observations across the two ADP formats are matched to known identities. Season rosters supply historical positions; Travis Hunter’s offensive production is assigned to WR despite the statistics file’s CB label. Current team/bye fields in the historical ADP API are ignored.

The primary curve uses 12-team two-QB mock prices, not an archived eight-team ESPN PPR superflex ranking. The API does not verify historical reception scoring. Both archived price formats are evaluated against actual full-PPR results. The red line is a nonincreasing least-squares fit of ten-player band means, interpolated between band centers; raw observations remain visible. Known ranks beyond 140 retain the last observed band’s value, while unranked players receive zero credit. Gray ranges are season ranges, not confidence intervals.

A 2026 player’s curve credit is the fitted value at his frozen ESPN rank, not a fresh projection of his performance. Subtract the credit at the actual pick and sum across offensive picks for acquisition surplus. Bench assets count toward acquisition but do not all enter a starting lineup. Curve credits do not change the published player projections. In hindsight the final top 30 and 40 supplied 79.4% and 89.9% of positive value; the preseason top 40 captured 58.8%.

Scoring and grades

Evan confirmed QB, two RB, two WR, TE, FLEX, SUPERFLEX, K, and D/ST, leaving six bench slots. Scoring assumes ESPN’s standard full PPR: one point per catch, one per ten rushing/receiving yards, six per rushing/receiving TD, one per 25 passing yards, four per passing TD, and minus two for interceptions or lost fumbles. A custom settings export was not supplied. ESPN standard scoring settings.

The index percentiles run from 0 to 100 within these eight teams; ties share their average rank. The balanced weights are 60/20/10/10. The downloadable calculations retain the previous index (60/20/20/0), plus starter-heavy (80/10/5/5), depth-heavy (40/40/10/10), value-heavy (35/15/25/25), and historical-value (60/20/0/20) alternatives. Letters reflect the written assessments and are not conversions of index scores into school percentages.

Clay’s published season totals determine offensive lineups. Totals divided by 17 are a common scale, not a matchup forecast. Reduced-game projections are retained without another haircut. Equal-weight starter-out scenarios test coverage, not injury likelihood. Optimistic pickups use the best projected undrafted player at the removed player’s position, without verifying current availability or competing claims. The three-WR sensitivity changes replacement demand and legal lineups.

K/D/ST appear in full rosters but are excluded from modeled point totals and curve credits. Their zero ESPN auction values are not a zero-points projection. ESPN’s default field-goal scale gives three points through 39 yards, four from 40–49, five from 50–59, and six from 60-plus. PATs earn one and missed field goals cost one. D/ST points include sacks, turnovers, touchdowns, points allowed, and yards allowed. ESPN standard scoring settings; ESPN defense and special teams scoring.
