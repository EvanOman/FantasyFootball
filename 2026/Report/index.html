Rome gets my top grade. Chase and St. Brown give this roster two of ESPN’s top four receivers, while McCaffrey, Love, and Hampton can fill both RB slots and FLEX. Sources, rules, and calculation details

Historical value

For each completed 2016–2025 regular season, the league-wide optimizer fills eight QB, sixteen RB, sixteen WR, eight TE, eight FLEX (RB/WR/TE), and eight SUPERFLEX slots. The next unselected player at each position defines replacement. Positive value is max(0, season PPR points − replacement points), divided by 16 scheduled games through 2020 and 17 thereafter. This includes the final NFL week and excludes playoffs. It is a full-season starter baseline, not a prediction of weekly waiver or streaming value.

Each season supplies 140 offensive preseason selections in ordinal ADP order. Injured and zero-production players remain in the sample. All 2,800 observations across the two ADP formats are matched to known identities. Season rosters supply historical positions; Travis Hunter’s offensive production is assigned to WR despite the statistics file’s CB label. Current team/bye fields in the historical ADP API are ignored.

The primary curve uses 12-team two-QB mock prices, not an archived eight-team ESPN PPR superflex ranking. The API does not verify historical reception scoring. Both price formats in the Lab are evaluated against actual full-PPR results. The red line is a nonincreasing least-squares fit of ten-player band means, interpolated between band centers; raw observations remain visible. Known ranks beyond 140 retain the last observed band’s value, while unranked players receive zero credit. Gray ranges are season ranges, not confidence intervals.

A 2026 player’s curve credit is the fitted value at his frozen ESPN rank, not a fresh projection of his performance. Subtract the credit at the actual pick and sum across offensive picks for acquisition surplus. Bench assets count toward acquisition but do not all enter a starting lineup. The curve does not change the Lab’s projected points or Elo-style power ratings. In hindsight the final top 30 and 40 supplied 79.4% and 89.9% of positive value; the preseason top 40 captured 58.8%.

Scoring and grades

Evan confirmed QB, two RB, two WR, TE, FLEX, SUPERFLEX, K, and D/ST, leaving six bench slots. Scoring assumes ESPN’s standard full PPR: one point per catch, one per ten rushing/receiving yards, six per rushing/receiving TD, one per 25 passing yards, four per passing TD, and minus two for interceptions or lost fumbles. A custom settings export was not supplied. ESPN standard scoring settings.

The index percentiles run from 0 to 100 within these eight teams; ties share their average rank. The balanced weights are 60/20/10/10. The Lab retains the previous index (60/20/20/0), plus starter-heavy (80/10/5/5), depth-heavy (40/40/10/10), value-heavy (35/15/25/25), and historical-value (60/20/0/20) alternatives. Letters reflect the written assessments and are not conversions of index scores into school percentages.

Clay’s published season totals determine offensive lineups. Totals divided by 17 are a common scale, not a matchup forecast. Reduced-game projections are retained without another haircut. Equal-weight starter-out scenarios test coverage, not injury likelihood. Optimistic pickups use the best projected undrafted player at the removed player’s position, without verifying current availability or competing claims. The three-WR sensitivity changes replacement demand and legal lineups.

K/D/ST appear in full rosters but are excluded from modeled point totals and curve credits. Their zero ESPN auction values are not a zero-points projection. ESPN’s default field-goal scale gives three points through 39 yards, four from 40–49, five from 50–59, and six from 60-plus. PATs earn one and missed field goals cost one. D/ST points include sacks, turnovers, touchdowns, points allowed, and yards allowed. ESPN standard scoring settings; ESPN defense and special teams scoring.

    What the chess-style number means

    Each lineup receives 1500 + 400 × log10(P / G), where P is its published projected point total and G is the geometric mean of all eight baseline totals in the selected lineup format. The baseline ratings average 1500. G stays fixed when an absence or bye is applied, so lost production remains visible in the ratings.

    A rating gap of D corresponds to a projected-point ratio of 10^(D / 400). The logistic expression used in US Chess’s standard expectancy formula (§4.2) therefore equals P₁ / (P₁ + P₂) here: a share of projected offense, not the likelihood of winning a fantasy matchup. No outcome history, weekly variance, player correlations, or new player-performance forecast enters this calculation. Small rating gaps are intentional; this league’s projected lineups are close.

    The scale is an analytical display choice, not a US Chess or ESPN fantasy model. It does not add information beyond the underlying points. For actual matchup odds, we would need independently validated weekly uncertainty estimates and the league’s full scoring settings.

    Data limits and the calculation

    Each year contributes 140 offensive players ordered by preseason ADP, with ten players per band. Rank here is the ordinal position among offensive players, not the decimal ADP pick itself. All ten seasons receive equal weight. The matched ADP windows end before the corresponding season opener; current team/bye fields from the historical ADP API are ignored.

    The pooled league optimizer fills mandatory slots plus FLEX and SUPERFLEX. Replacement is the next unselected player at each position, using full regular-season totals, including the final NFL week. Value below this baseline is floored at zero because a manager can replace an unproductive asset. This idealized full-season comparison cannot capture weekly streaming, schedule effects, lineup mistakes, or the cost of finding a replacement.

    Band means are fitted with weighted nonincreasing least squares (pool-adjacent-violators), then linearly interpolated between band centers. The fit is a descriptive rank-price scale, not a validated individual forecast. Identical early ranks within the first band and late ranks beyond the sample cannot be distinguished reliably. Historical positions use season rosters; Travis Hunter’s offensive points are assigned to WR despite the results file’s CB label. Zero-production drafted players remain included.
